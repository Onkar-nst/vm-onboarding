# Outreach Email Templates

Two cold-outreach templates (one per app), a follow-up sequence, and the
personalization formula. Written to be sent by a human, one company at a time
or in small personalized batches.

> **Important — sending at scale:** don't blast 500 identical emails in one go.
> Bulk unsolicited email gets your domain flagged as spam, and India's consent
> norms + platform rules punish it. Send 20–40/day, personalize the first line
> for each company, use your real name and address, and include a working
> opt-out line. Quality beats volume in B2B outreach anyway.

## Personalization formula (fill these 3 slots per company)

1. `{{first_line_hook}}` — one specific, true sentence about them
   (from the "why it fits" column of the prospect list, or their site/Instagram):
   *"Saw that you ran three wedding crews in parallel last weekend on Instagram."*
2. `{{pain_number}}` — a number that makes the pain concrete for their segment
   (a lost FX3 body ≈ ₹2,00,000; a new editor's unproductive first week ≈ one
   full salary week; double-booked gear = one cancelled shoot).
3. `{{sender_block}}` — your name, company, phone, city. Real and consistent.

## Template A — Gear Wall (production houses, studios, wedding filmers, rental houses)

**Subject options:**
- Who has your FX3 right now?
- {{company}} — your gear room, on one screen
- Stop losing shoot days to missing equipment

**Body:**

Hi {{first_name / team {{company}}}},

{{first_line_hook}}

When multiple crews share one equipment room, the same three things keep
happening: nobody knows who took the camera, two shoots get booked on the same
kit, and a {{pain_number}} body goes "missing" until someone checks a cupboard
three weeks later.

We built **Gear Wall** to fix exactly this. It's a simple web app your whole
team uses in the browser:

- **The Wall** — every camera, lens, light and mic with live status: available,
  in use (by whom, for which shoot, for how long), or damaged.
- **Booking calendar with approvals** — crews request gear for a date range;
  clashes with other bookings are flagged before they happen; a manager
  approves in one click.
- **QR pickup & return** — a sticker on each item; scan on the way out, scan
  on the way back. Condition is recorded both ways, overdue returns get
  flagged automatically, and you get a full usage history per item.

It takes about an hour to set up your inventory, works on any phone or laptop,
and your team needs zero training — if they can scan a QR at a restaurant,
they can use this.

Worth a 15-minute demo this week? Reply with a time, or I can send a link to
a live demo you can click around yourself.

{{sender_block}}

*If gear tracking isn't your problem, reply "no thanks" and I won't write again.*

## Template B — Onboarding Academy (marketing / creative / digital agencies)

**Subject options:**
- New joiners productive on day one — without a senior babysitting them
- {{company}} — turn your process doc into an academy
- Your onboarding doc, but people actually finish it

**Body:**

Hi {{first_name / team {{company}}}},

{{first_line_hook}}

Every agency has the same onboarding problem: the process lives in a long
document nobody reads, so every new joiner costs a senior person a full day
of repeating the same things — and you still discover the gaps three weeks
later, in front of a client.

We built the **Onboarding Academy** to fix this. You paste in the processes
you've already written, and it becomes an interactive course:

- **Short modules that unlock one by one** — each ends with a quick-check
  question, so you *know* they read it (wrong answers are logged, so you also
  know exactly what they didn't get).
- **A final quiz and a real day-one assignment** — they do one actual piece
  of work against your checklist and submit it for review.
- **Leads review only the gaps** — the app shows exactly which checklist items
  failed, you write feedback on those alone, and they resubmit. New joiners
  contribute real work in week one, not week four.

A course takes an afternoon to build in the built-in editor (no IT needed),
and you can run different tracks for designers, writers, and account teams.

Worth a 15-minute demo? Reply with a time, or I'll send a live link you can
try yourself.

{{sender_block}}

*Not the right fit? Reply "no thanks" and I won't write again.*

## Follow-up sequence (send only if no reply)

**Follow-up 1 (day 3–4):** one sentence + the strongest artifact.
> Hi {{first_name}} — sharing a 60-second screen recording of {{app}} in case
> the last mail got buried: {{link}}. Would love your take.

**Follow-up 2 (day 8–10):** the "case" angle, then close the thread.
> Hi {{first_name}} — last note from me. Teams like yours use {{app}} to
> {{one-line outcome: "cut gear losses to zero" / "make new editors productive
> in week one"}}. If it's ever relevant, this thread has the details. All the best!

Stop after two follow-ups. A "no" today is a warm door in six months.

## Sending checklist

- [ ] First line is specific to this company (never generic)
- [ ] Their name/city spelled correctly
- [ ] One app per email — never pitch both at once
- [ ] Sent from a real personal address (not info@/noreply@)
- [ ] Opt-out line present
- [ ] Logged in your tracker (company, date, template, response)
